What kind of decision maker are you?

25 statements about how you make important decisions, based on the General Decision-Making Style (GDMS) questionnaire by Scott and Bruce (1995).

Think about important decisions in your life, not everyday choices. For each statement, say how much you agree. There are no right or wrong answers, and nothing you enter is saved or sent anywhere.

Your strongest style is

Scott and Bruce found that people use a combination of styles rather than just one (p. 829). Read your result as a profile across all five styles. Your highest score shows the style you lean on most, not the only one you use.

Items and style definitions are from Scott and Bruce (1995). The statements come from Table 1 (pp. 825–826); the response format from pp. 821–822; the definitions of the rational, intuitive, dependent and avoidant styles from p. 820; and the definition of the spontaneous style from p. 823. Table 1 prints four rational items, although the text reports five per scale (p. 824). The fifth rational item used here, "I plan my important decisions carefully," comes from later published versions of the GDMS (e.g., Bodin, Chermack & Coons, 2016).

Scores are the average of the items for each style, from 1 to 5. This quiz is for learning and self-reflection. It is not a clinical or diagnostic tool.
